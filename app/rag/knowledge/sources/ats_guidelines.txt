ATS Resume Optimization Guidelines

Purpose

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) commonly process resumes by extracting text, identifying sections, and matching terminology against job requirements. Resume optimization should improve machine-readable relevance without sacrificing factual accuracy or readability.

Keyword Usage

Use terminology from the job description when it accurately describes the candidate's existing skills, tools, technologies, responsibilities, or qualifications.

Prefer exact or commonly recognized terminology when the candidate genuinely has the corresponding skill or experience.

Do not add a technology, certification, responsibility, job title, or qualification merely because it appears in a job description.

Avoid unnecessary keyword repetition. Keywords should appear naturally in relevant resume sections.

Skill Matching

Compare the candidate's documented skills against the requirements of the target job.

Distinguish between:
- Explicitly stated skills
- Closely related terminology
- Skills that are not supported by the resume

Only explicitly supported candidate skills should be presented as possessed skills.

Section Structure

Use recognizable section headings such as:
- Summary
- Skills
- Experience
- Projects
- Education
- Certifications

Consistent section headings help automated systems and human reviewers identify resume content.

Formatting

Prefer simple, machine-readable formatting.

Avoid relying on images, graphics, text embedded inside images, decorative elements, or complex layouts to communicate important information.

Use consistent formatting for dates, titles, organizations, technologies, and bullet points.

Content Relevance

Prioritize information that is relevant to the target job.

Relevant skills, technologies, responsibilities, projects, qualifications, and measurable achievements should receive appropriate emphasis when supported by the candidate's resume.

Do not remove important factual information solely to increase keyword density.

Accuracy and Grounding

Resume optimization must preserve factual accuracy.

A job description is evidence of what an employer is seeking, not evidence that the candidate possesses those requirements.

General ATS guidance may explain how to present information, but it must never be used as evidence of candidate experience.

Missing Skills

When a required skill is absent from the candidate's resume, identify it as a gap rather than adding it to the resume as an existing skill.

Related skills may be emphasized when they are genuinely present, but related terminology must not be represented as direct experience unless the resume supports that interpretation.

Keyword Placement

When a supported keyword is relevant, it can be naturally incorporated into:
- Professional summary
- Skills section
- Experience bullets
- Project descriptions
- Other appropriate resume sections

The placement should reflect the candidate's actual background.

Resume Quality

ATS optimization should balance:
- Relevance
- Accuracy
- Readability
- Consistent structure
- Appropriate keyword usage
- Factual grounding

Optimization should improve discoverability while preserving the truth of the candidate's background.
