Resume Writing and Optimization Guidelines

Purpose

A strong resume should present the candidate's qualifications clearly, concisely, and accurately while making relevant information easy to identify.

Professional Summary

The professional summary should provide a concise overview of the candidate's relevant background, skills, technologies, qualifications, and career focus.

The summary should prioritize information relevant to the target role.

Do not introduce skills, experience, achievements, certifications, or qualifications that are not supported by the candidate's resume.

Skills Section

The skills section should contain technologies, tools, programming languages, frameworks, platforms, methodologies, and other capabilities that the candidate genuinely possesses.

Group related skills when doing so improves readability.

Prioritize skills that are relevant to the target job while retaining important supported skills.

Do not add missing job requirements as candidate skills.

Experience Section

Experience descriptions should communicate the candidate's actual responsibilities, contributions, technologies, and outcomes.

Use concise bullet points.

Begin bullets with clear action-oriented language when appropriate.

Where measurable results are genuinely available, present them clearly.

Do not invent metrics, responsibilities, employers, job titles, dates, or achievements.

Project Section

Projects can demonstrate practical application of technologies and concepts, particularly when professional experience is limited.

Project descriptions should identify:
- Project purpose
- Technologies used
- Relevant implementation details
- Meaningful outcomes when supported

Projects should only claim work that the candidate actually performed.

Achievement-Oriented Writing

When supported by the source resume, describe work in terms of action, technical contribution, and result.

A useful structure is:

Action + technical contribution + outcome

For example, a candidate may describe how they implemented a system, used a technology, analyzed data, or improved an existing process when those activities are supported by their documented background.

Do not manufacture outcomes merely to make a bullet appear stronger.

Keyword Alignment

Use terminology from the target job description when it accurately corresponds to the candidate's existing background.

Equivalent terminology may be useful when the relationship is clear and factually supported.

Do not transform a related skill into an unsupported claim of direct experience.

Content Prioritization

Give greater emphasis to information that is:
- Relevant to the target position
- Supported by the candidate's resume
- Technically meaningful
- Recent or substantial when applicable
- Useful for demonstrating qualifications

Less relevant information can receive less emphasis, but factual information should not be altered.

Length and Conciseness

Resume content should be concise and avoid unnecessary repetition.

Remove redundant wording when the same information is already communicated effectively elsewhere.

Do not remove important qualifications, projects, skills, or achievements solely to reduce length.

Consistency

Maintain consistent:
- Section headings
- Date formats
- Job-title formatting
- Organization names
- Bullet styles
- Technology names
- Capitalization

Consistency improves readability and makes information easier to process.

Formatting

Prefer clean and machine-readable layouts.

Important information should be represented as selectable text rather than images.

Avoid unnecessary decorative elements that interfere with text extraction or readability.

Factual Integrity

Resume optimization must preserve the candidate's factual record.

The following must not be fabricated:
- Skills
- Work experience
- Job titles
- Employers
- Dates
- Certifications
- Degrees
- Responsibilities
- Projects
- Metrics
- Achievements

A job description describes employer requirements. It does not establish that the candidate satisfies those requirements.

Skill Gaps

When a job requirement is not supported by the candidate's resume, treat it as a skill or qualification gap.

Do not silently add the missing requirement to the resume.

When appropriate, related existing skills may be emphasized without misrepresenting them as the missing skill.

Final Review

Before producing an optimized resume, verify that:
- Relevant information is easy to find.
- Job-relevant supported skills are appropriately emphasized.
- The resume remains factually accurate.
- No unsupported claims were introduced.
- Repetition has been minimized.
- Formatting is consistent.
- Content remains readable.
- The final resume reflects the candidate's actual background.
