Job Role and Requirement Alignment Guidelines

Purpose

Role alignment helps connect the requirements of a target position with the candidate's documented background. The goal is to emphasize relevant qualifications without introducing unsupported claims.

Job Description Analysis

A job description may contain:
- Required skills
- Preferred skills
- Technologies and tools
- Responsibilities
- Qualifications
- Experience requirements
- Domain knowledge
- Keywords and terminology

These requirements describe the employer's expectations for the target role.

Requirement Classification

Classify job requirements according to their relationship with the candidate's documented background:

- Direct match: the candidate explicitly demonstrates the requirement.
- Related match: the candidate has closely related knowledge or technology that may be relevant.
- Gap: the requirement is not supported by the candidate's resume.

Direct matches can be emphasized confidently.

Related matches should be described accurately and should not automatically be presented as equivalent to the requested requirement.

Gaps should remain gaps unless additional verified candidate information supports the requirement.

Skill Alignment

Compare target-role skills with the skills documented in the candidate's resume.

When terminology differs but the underlying capability is clearly supported, consistent terminology may improve clarity.

Do not infer substantial expertise from a loosely related technology.

Technology Alignment

Technology names should be preserved accurately.

For example, familiarity with one programming language, framework, database, cloud platform, or machine learning tool should not automatically be represented as experience with another technology.

Related technologies may be highlighted as adjacent skills when the resume supports that relationship.

Responsibility Alignment

Target responsibilities can guide which existing resume content receives greater emphasis.

If the candidate has documented work or project experience corresponding to a responsibility, that experience may be prioritized.

If the responsibility is not supported by the resume, it must not be presented as something the candidate has performed.

Experience Requirements

Experience requirements in a job description should be compared with the candidate's documented experience.

Do not create employment history, years of experience, seniority, job titles, or responsibilities to satisfy a requirement.

For candidates with project-based or academic experience, those experiences may be presented as projects or academic work rather than being represented as professional employment.

Domain Alignment

Relevant domain knowledge can be emphasized when it is explicitly supported by the candidate's background.

A target industry's presence in a job description does not establish that the candidate has experience in that industry.

Missing domain experience should remain clearly distinguishable from demonstrated experience.

Keyword Alignment

Target-role terminology can be used when it accurately represents information already present in the candidate's resume.

Keywords should be placed in context rather than inserted as isolated or repetitive terms.

Keyword optimization must not override factual accuracy.

Prioritization

When tailoring a resume to a role, prioritize supported information that has the strongest relevance to the target requirements.

Possible areas for prioritization include:
- Relevant technical skills
- Relevant projects
- Relevant responsibilities
- Relevant qualifications
- Relevant achievements
- Relevant tools and technologies

Prioritization changes emphasis, not the underlying facts.

Transferable Skills

Transferable skills may be emphasized when the resume provides evidence for them.

Examples may include:
- Data analysis
- Problem solving
- Software development
- Communication
- Project execution
- Research
- Analytical thinking

The wording should remain proportional to the evidence available in the candidate's resume.

Gap Identification

A missing requirement should be identified as a gap rather than silently incorporated into the resume.

Useful gap categories include:
- Missing technical skill
- Missing tool or platform
- Missing qualification
- Missing experience
- Missing domain knowledge
- Missing responsibility evidence

Gap information can guide recommendations without becoming candidate claims.

Tailoring Principle

Resume tailoring should answer:

"What existing candidate information is most relevant to this role?"

It should not answer:

"What information can be invented to make the candidate appear to satisfy the role?"

Grounding Principle

Candidate claims must be grounded in candidate-provided information.

Job descriptions provide requirements.

Knowledge-base documents provide general optimization guidance.

Neither a job description nor general knowledge-base guidance should be treated as evidence that the candidate possesses a skill, qualification, responsibility, or experience.

Final Role Alignment Review

Before finalizing a tailored resume, verify that:
- Target requirements were identified correctly.
- Supported matches receive appropriate emphasis.
- Related skills remain accurately described.
- Gaps are not presented as existing qualifications.
- Candidate experience has not been fabricated.
- Job-description terminology is used only when factually appropriate.
- The final content remains faithful to the candidate's documented background.
